# Irimi-nage

**Japanese:** 入り身投げ (いりみなげ)
**English:** Entering throw
**Attack:** Shomen-uchi, Yokomen-uchi, Katate-dori, Ushiro attacks
**Category:** Throw (nage-waza)
**First required at:** [[6th Kyu (Rokkyu)]]

---

## Description

Irimi-nage is one of Aikido's most characteristic techniques — a blending entry that places nage behind or alongside uke, then throws with a sweeping arm motion to the neck/shoulder. O-Sensei reportedly called it "the 20-year technique" because true mastery takes a long time. The entry (irimi) is everything.

---

## Key points

- The irimi (entering step) must be decisive — commit to the space next to uke
- After entry, get your shoulder next to uke's — you should be looking the same direction they are
- The throw comes from your hips and body weight, not the arm swing
- Uke's neck/shoulder receives the throw — don't push the head

## Common mistakes

- Timid entry — not going deep enough alongside uke
- Using the arm to throw instead of whole body movement
- Rushing the throw before achieving the correct position
- Losing connection with uke during the entry phase

---

## Omote (front entry)

Blend with the attack, step across to the outside, and bring uke around you in a small circle. As they come around, your leading arm guides across their neck/shoulder. Follow through with body weight into the throw.

## Ura (rear entry)

Use tenkan (pivot) to blend. Uke goes around in a wider arc. Same final position and throw as omote, different path to get there.
